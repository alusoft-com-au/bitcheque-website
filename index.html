hand-signed.
Quantum-proof.

Bitcheque is an experimental proof-of-work blockchain. Print your cheque book offline, split across two printers, so neither printer ever sees a whole cheque. After that you can sign and send from any computer without trusting it. Open source under the MIT licence.

The device was the weak point.
So we removed it.

A device can be tampered with before it reaches you, or changed by an update. Any design that keeps your key on one device can fail that way. Once your book is printed, Bitcheque keeps the key off every device.

Print offline. Move up to 255 times.

About 20 minutes offline, then register from any computer. Two hours later your account number is final.

01

Print offline

On a clean, offline computer, make your seed and print the book in two parts, on two printers. Save the public registration file, then switch everything off.
02

Register online

Take the registration file to any online computer and open it on this site. A minute or two of proof-of-work registers the book. The file holds no secrets.
03

Get your number

About two hours later, scan the QR code on your sheet to see your final account number. Give it out to receive.
04

Sign by hand

Write the recipient and amount, then copy or scan that cheque's 18 boxes on any computer. Your signed cheque is safe anywhere: no one can change who receives it or how much.

Write a cheque.

Enter the recipient and amount. The lit box in each column is the one to copy or scan from your cheque. Then broadcast.

Send from which account?

The number printed on this cheque page.

This browser keeps a record of each cheque it sends, so one cheque is never sent for two different recipients or amounts. A private or incognito window forgets that record when it closes: send from a normal window.

warning

Three things you don't have to trust.

No device

No device sees your seed while you sign: the computer only gets that cheque's 18 boxes. The book is printed offline and the machines are switched off: a printer that never touches a network has nowhere to send it.

No curves

The chain stores one hash per cell and compares. No signature algorithm, no quantum shortcut: 2^128 classical, 2^64 sequential quantum steps.

No memory

The chain records which cheques are used. Lose the book, reprint from the seed. No counter, file or app state to back up.

Run a node
in one command.

The node is Rust with no dependencies, down to its own SHA-256. Consensus is small enough to audit line by line.

Straight answers.

The questions people ask most about safety, loss and mining.

01Is it really quantum-safe?add

Yes, at the level NIST accepts. Forging a cell needs a SHA-256 preimage: 2^128 work classically, 2^64 sequential steps on a quantum computer. There are no elliptic curves in consensus.

02What if a printer is hacked?add

Print on a clean, offline computer with USB printers, then switch everything off. A printer that never touches a network has nowhere to send what it printed. Your book is printed in two parts, ideally on two printers, so neither printer ever sees a whole cheque.

03What if I lose a book?add

Reprint your highest registered cheque from the seed and use it to sweep everything to a new book: that closes every unused cheque of the lost one. You only lose the account if the seed and the book are both gone.

04Is it safer than Bitcoin?add

It is a different trade. Bitcoin's weak point is the signing device and, later, quantum computers. Bitcheque's weak point is paper: whoever holds an unused cheque can make one move, so keep the book somewhere safe.

05Is there a token sale?add

No. This is an experimental research project: we do not sell coins, and they carry no promise of value. Anyone who chooses to buy, sell or trade them does so at their own risk, outside our control. While the network is new the chain may be reset at any time.

06Why isn't my account number shown right away?add

Account numbers are short so you can copy them by hand, and they are handed out in the order books are registered. The newest few blocks can still be replaced by others, and then the same number could land on a different book. So a new number stays hidden for 100 blocks, about 100 minutes, until the order is settled for good. Scan the code on your sheet, or type its lookup code on the explorer, to see your number once it is final. The chain also refuses any cheque to a number that is not final yet, so nothing can be sent to it too early.

07Who can see what I do?add

The chain is public: anyone can see every transfer of an account number. The node your browser connects to also sees your IP address and every account, lookup code and cheque you ask it about, so it can link your internet connection to your account. Use a node you trust, run your own, or connect through a VPN or Tor. This site needs no account, collects no name or email, and only remembers a few settings in your own browser.
